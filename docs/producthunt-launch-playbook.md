## 2. How Product Hunt Works

### The Ranking Algorithm

Product Hunt does not publicly disclose its exact algorithm, but the community has reverse-engineered the key factors:

| Factor | Weight | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Weighted Voting** | High | Not all upvotes are equal. Votes from established PH users with history carry more weight than brand-new accounts. |
| **Vote Velocity** | High | A burst of votes in the first 1-4 hours matters enormously. Early momentum determines your trajectory for the day. |
| **Voter Quality** | High | Votes from accounts that regularly use PH (upvote, comment, hunt) count more. Votes from accounts created that day may count for almost nothing. |
| **Engagement** | Medium | Comments, replies, and discussion signal genuine interest. Products with active comment threads rank higher. |
| **Maker Engagement** | Medium | Founders who actively reply to every comment get a ranking boost. Responsiveness matters. |
| **Unique Visitors** | Low-Medium | Traffic to your PH page is a secondary signal. |

### Key Mechanics

- **The Day resets at 12:01 AM Pacific Time (PT).** Your product competes only against others launched that same day.
- **The leaderboard is live all day.** Position fluctuates until roughly midnight PT when final rankings lock.
- **Top 5 finishers** earn a "Product of the Day" badge (with rank: #1, #2, etc.) — this is the primary goal.
- **Top product of the week/month** badges also exist but are secondary targets.
- **Products can only launch once.** You get one shot. (Re-launches are possible for major version updates, e.g., v2.0.)

### Best Launch Days

| Day | Rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday** | Best | High traffic, reasonable competition |
| **Wednesday** | Best | High traffic, reasonable competition |
| **Thursday** | Good | Slightly less traffic |
| **Monday** | Okay | People are catching up from weekend |
| **Friday** | Avoid | Traffic drops off in afternoon |
| **Sat/Sun** | Avoid | Lowest traffic, but also lowest competition — risky |

### Timing in WIB (Jakarta/Indonesian Western Time)

Since PT is 14 hours behind WIB (during PDT) or 15 hours behind (during PST):

| Event (PT) | WIB Equivalent |
|---|---|
| 12:01 AM PT — Day starts | 2:01 PM WIB (same day) |
| 6:00 AM PT — Early morning push | 8:00 PM WIB |
| 12:00 PM PT — Midday peak | 2:00 AM WIB (next day) |
| 6:00 PM PT — Evening | 8:00 AM WIB (next day) |
| 11:59 PM PT — Day ends | 1:59 PM WIB (next day) |

> **Important:** This means launch day for you starts on a Tuesday afternoon WIB and runs through Wednesday afternoon WIB. Plan sleep accordingly.

## 4. Maker Comment

The Maker Comment is the first comment on your product page. It is posted immediately at launch (12:01 AM PT). It should be personal, authentic, and tell the story behind the app.

### Pre-Written Maker Comment

```markdown
Hey Product Hunt! 👋

I'm the solo developer behind IdeaTamer, and I built it because I was drowning
in my own ideas.

My notes app had 50+ "brilliant" ideas. I'd start something new every week,
abandon it when the next shiny thing appeared, and end up with a graveyard of
half-built projects. I tried every productivity app out there — they all gave
me more lists and more ways to organize. But organizing wasn't my problem.
**Finishing** was my problem.

So I asked myself: what if picking an idea and sticking with it felt like a
game? What if I could compete against myself — not in a stressful way, but in
the way that makes you want to beat your own high score?

That's IdeaTamer.

**How it works:**
1. Dump all your ideas into the app (Quick Capture — takes seconds)
2. Score each one on Impact, Effort, and Alignment
3. The best idea becomes your **Quest** — your ONE active mission
4. Each week, you Duel against your past self: did you make progress?
5. Earn XP, level up, unlock badges, build streaks

The ideas you actually finish go into your **Hall of Fame** — a trophy room of
shipped work.

**A few things I'm proud of:**
- It's **completely free**. No subscriptions, no IAP, no ads.
- It works **fully offline**. No server, no cloud, no sync headaches.
- **Zero data collection**. I literally don't know who uses this app, and that's
  by design.
- No account needed. Open it and start.

I'd love to hear what you think. I'm here all day answering questions.

What's the idea YOU'VE been putting off? 👇
```

> **Note:** The closing question is intentional — it invites engagement, which boosts the ranking algorithm. Every reply you get (and respond to) helps.

## 8. What NOT To Do

### Disqualification & Penalty Risks

| Action | Risk Level | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Buying upvotes** | CRITICAL | Permanent ban from Product Hunt |
| **Using upvote exchange services/groups** | HIGH | Votes nullified, potential ban |
| **Asking for upvotes on the PH page itself** | HIGH | PH moderators may flag your product |
| **Coordinated identical comments** | HIGH | Comments removed, votes discounted |
| **Creating fake accounts to upvote** | CRITICAL | All votes from those IPs nullified, potential ban |
| **Sending direct PH links with "please upvote"** | MEDIUM | Votes may be discounted if pattern detected |
| **Launching on someone else's launch day without checking** | LOW | Not a penalty, but check if a major product is launching the same day |

### Common Mistakes

1. **Launching before your audience is ready.** If you have <100 Coming Soon subscribers and <50 people in your launch team, you are not ready. Wait.

2. **Ignoring comments on launch day.** Unanswered comments signal low maker engagement. The algorithm notices. Your potential supporters notice.

3. **Posting only once on social media.** One tweet is not enough. You need multiple posts throughout the day, each with a different angle.

4. **Asking friends with new PH accounts to upvote.** Votes from brand-new accounts (especially created on launch day) carry almost zero weight and can trigger spam detection. It's counterproductive.

5. **Over-focusing on the number of upvotes.** Vote quality matters more than quantity. 100 votes from established PH users will outrank 300 votes from new accounts.

6. **Launching on a day when a major company is also launching.** Check the PH upcoming page the week before. If Apple, Google, or a well-funded startup with a huge following is launching the same day, pick a different day.

7. **Not having the product available.** On launch day, people want to try your app. If the App Store link is not live, you will lose significant momentum. Make sure IdeaTamer is approved and published **before** you launch on PH.

8. **Writing a generic maker comment.** "We built something cool, check it out!" tells nobody anything. Your maker comment should be personal, specific, and tell a story.

9. **Disappearing after launch day.** The 48 hours after launch still drive traffic. Stay active on PH, reply to new comments, and keep sharing on social media.

10. **Setting up the PH page at the last minute.** Have everything — description, images, video, links — finalized and uploaded at least 3 days before launch. Technical issues on launch day are devastating.

```

Include:

- Traffic numbers (PH page views → website visits → App Store taps)
- Email signups gained

```

**Why:** The #buildinpublic community loves transparency. This post often generates more followers than the launch itself, and sets you up well for future launches.

### Re-Launch for v2.0

Product Hunt allows re-launches for major updates. Plan for this:

- Track feature requests from PH comments — they tell you what to build next
- When v2.0 is ready (with significant new features), you can launch again
- Second launches benefit from your existing PH reputation and a larger audience

### Convert PH Traffic

PH traffic is a spike, not a steady stream. Capture it:

1. **App Store optimization** — make sure your App Store listing is polished before PH launch
2. **Email capture** — ensure ideatamer.app is converting visitors to email subscribers
3. **Social follows** — your Twitter CTA should be prominent on the landing page
4. **Retargeting** — if you ever run ads in the future, PH launch day visitors are your warmest audience

### Ongoing PH Presence

- Continue engaging with the PH community after launch
- Your maker profile now has a launched product — this gives you credibility
- Support other makers' launches — reciprocity pays off long-term

## 10. IdeaTamer Competitive Advantages for PH

Product Hunt audiences see hundreds of productivity apps. Here is why IdeaTamer stands out and how to communicate each advantage:

### 1. Gamification Angle (Primary Differentiator)

**Why it matters:** Most productivity apps are serious, sterile, and forgettable. IdeaTamer has XP, levels, streaks, badges, quests, and duels. PH users love novel approaches to solved problems.

**How to communicate it:**
- Lead with "gamified" in your tagline and description
- Gallery images should show game-like UI elements prominently
- Use gaming language: "quest," "duel," "level up," "XP," "Hall of Fame"

### 2. "Compete With Yourself" Hook

**Why it matters:** Competition apps usually mean competing with others (leaderboards, social pressure). Competing with *yourself* is unexpected, healthier, and more interesting to talk about.

**How to communicate it:**
- "Your opponent is last week's you" is a quotable line — use it
- The Weekly Duel feature should be explained clearly with visuals
- This angle resonates strongly with solo founders and indie makers (PH's core audience)

### 3. Free Forever, No Account, Offline

**Why it matters:** PH users are deeply skeptical of "free" products because they expect hidden monetization. IdeaTamer genuinely has none. This is remarkable and worth emphasizing.

**How to communicate it:**
- State it explicitly: "No subscriptions. No in-app purchases. No ads. Ever."
- Explain *why* it's free (indie passion project, not a VC-funded growth play)
- The "no account" feature removes all friction — highlight the instant onboarding

### 4. Privacy-First (Zero Data Collection)

**Why it matters:** Privacy is a growing concern and a strong selling point, especially in the productivity space where apps routinely harvest user data. Zero data collection is a bold claim that stands out.

**How to communicate it:**
- "We don't know who uses this app. By design."
- Reference the App Store privacy label (if it shows "No Data Collected")
- This pairs well with "offline-first" — there is literally no server to send data to

### 5. Built for iOS 26

**Why it matters:** At launch time (September 2026), iOS 26 will be brand new. Being among the first apps built exclusively for it generates curiosity and signals technical ambition.

**How to communicate it:**
- "Built from the ground up for iOS 26" — implies modern architecture
- If there are specific iOS 26 features you leverage, highlight them
- This attracts Apple enthusiasts and tech-forward PH users

### 6. Indie Developer Story

**Why it matters:** PH loves indie makers. The community was built on small teams shipping creative products. A solo developer with a genuine personal problem is the quintessential PH origin story.

**How to communicate it:**
- Your maker comment should be personal and vulnerable
- "I had too many ideas and finished none. So I built a game to fix myself."
- Engage in comments as a real person, not a brand
